## Safety Phrasing Rules

- Never state that a normal radiograph rules out a high-risk injury. Use language such as "radiographs may be normal early" or "negative films do not exclude this when clinical suspicion is high."
- Separate radiographic recognition from clinical management. Use "urgent referral," "protect weightbearing," or "advanced imaging" only where the curriculum source supports it.
- Avoid absolute timelines unless a source gives the range. Prefer "often," "can," or "typically" when individual care varies.
- For pediatric injuries, explicitly protect the growth plate and avoid implying that a normal film excludes physeal injury when focal physeal tenderness is present.
- For comparison images, label which panel is normal and which panel is abnormal in the caption, alt text, or nearby teaching copy.
- For do-not-miss entities, include the consequence of the miss: instability, avascular necrosis, growth arrest, nonunion, occult fracture, or neurovascular risk.

## Content Review Checklist

- High-risk negative X-ray pathways mention the next step: repeat imaging, MRI/advanced imaging, immobilization/protected weightbearing, or referral.
- Quiz explanations teach why the correct answer is correct and why at least one tempting alternative is wrong.
- Case diagnosis options have one defensible best answer, and the explanation does not depend on hidden information absent from the stem/image caption.
- Cheat sheet escalation language matches the module page and does not create conflicting advice.
- Image captions and alt text identify the view and the key finding without overcalling certainty beyond the image.
- Pediatric content distinguishes normal ossification variants from fracture/dislocation patterns.
- Any new high-risk claim gets a source anchor added here before release.
